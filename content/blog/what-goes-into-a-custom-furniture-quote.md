A custom furniture quote looks like one price. The hours behind it are a sequence of ordinary checks.

## What the quote has to hold

A usable quote rests on facts the shop can check:

- The drawing the client sent
- The materials that job actually uses
- The labor that job actually takes
- What is included, and what is not
- The questions that come back before anyone approves it

If one of those is missing, the number on the page is a draft. Someone still has to go find the missing piece.

## Where the hours go

The hours are not in choosing a model. They are in the passes that repeat on every job:

- Reading the drawing and listing what has to be built
- Looking up material costs and what is already on hand
- Estimating labor from how the shop builds that kind of piece
- Writing the quote so a client can read it
- Rewriting it when the drawing changes
- Answering the questions that come back
- Keeping the status of the job after a person approves the price

None of these are a strategy. They are hours. Hours are the cost.

A person still approves the price. The draft can be prepared for that person. It should not be sent, and the price should not be promised, until that person approves it.
